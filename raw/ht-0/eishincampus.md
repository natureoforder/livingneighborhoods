<td colspan="2" style="text-align: left;" width="100%" data-valign="top"><span class="h404class"> PROJECT HISTORY</span> <span class="h4class"><span class="h4class">Eishin Gakuen, an innovative private high school, was originally located in Musashino-shi, outside Tokyo. They decided to build a new school which was to become a combination high-school and college, on a new site of 9 hectares, once tea bush land, outside Tokyo, in Iruma-shi, Saitama Prefecture.</span></span>
<p>The architectural commission to build this community, came with the explicit insistence, by the managing director of the school, Hisae Hosoi, that he wanted the project to be done under conditions where faculty, staff, and students, were all taking part in the design process. And by this he meant, not the pablum of token "participation" and "charettes" that has become common in the last twenty years, but honest-to-goodness decision making by the people in the school, based on individual and group understanding.</p>
<p>Mr. Murakoshi, the school's vice-principal, went further. He told me that the school, as a whole, wanted to rebuild their own culture, in such a way as to build deeper allegience and practice to humane principles -- and he want me to take care, as far as it was possible, that the physical layout and spiritual cooperation of the members of the school, would have this effect: the creation of a new culture.</p>

<span class="caption">Laying out the campus, with flags, so that we could all, together, feel the buildings and the spaces of the neighborhood, adjust them, again and again, until they felt just right.</span></p></td>

<span class="h4class">**POINTS OF SPECIAL INTEREST IN THIS PROJECT**</span>

Of all the projects we have done, this is one where the development of the pattern language and generative process received the most complete, and formal process of discovery of patterns, experiments, modification and formulation of patterns, hammering out a final version in committee, and then formal ratification of our final draft by the body of the whole school: directors, teachers, staff and students.\
