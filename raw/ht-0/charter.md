<td style="text-align: left;" width="450" data-valign="top"><span class="h3class" style="color:black">This first draft of a charter for professional developers, arose from two impulses. First, a number of years ago, Andres Duany asked me if I would be willing to draft such a charter, and if so, proposed that he would then take it to a number of influential developers and press for its adoption. At the time, I felt I was not quite ready to undertake this task.</span>
<p>Then, more recently, at the 14th Congress of New Urbanism, a very considerable number of architects, planners, developers and builders, met with me after my address to the congress. Together we decided that we would pursue this concept, probably at a meeting held in London, with the idea that we would then thrash out the details of the articles together.</p>
<p>The version presented below is a very rough draft which I made , virtually on the back of an envelope, and then showed at the Congress. It is only a starting point. Many items, especially the items 9-23, need considerable review and refinement, before they could reasonably be accepted by a wide group of charter members.<br />
                                                                                            Christopher Alexander</p></td>

<span class="h3class">Version 1, June 3 2006. Very rough first draft</span></td>

<tr>
<td style="text-align: left;" width="450" data-valign="top"><span class="h5class" style="color:red"><u>The Primary Article of Intent</u></span>
<p><span class="h8class" style="color:black">To Protect and Care for the Beauty of the Living Earth</span></p>
<p><span class="h5class" style="color:red"><u>Secondary Articles of Intent that Express and Manifest this Primary Concern.</u></span><span class="h8class" style="color:black"></span></p>
<ol>
<li>Recognize that we are responsible for the Earth.</li>
<li>Pay attention to the beauty of the whole, and always act in service to the whole.</li>
<li>Do no harm.</li>
<li>Base everything on unfolding.</li>
<li>Make this a work of love, not commercial.</li>
<li>Spend as much time as possible on site.</li>
<li>Involve people.</li>
<li>Trust the judgment of others, and believe in them as you believe in yourself.</li>
</ol>
<span class="h5class" style="color:red"><u>Tertiary Practical Articles<br />
The Methods and Assumptions which we believe any Developer will need to Subscribe to, in order to act practically on his/her Devotion to the Earth.</u></span>
<ol start="9">
<li>Recognize yourself as a fee-based professional.</li>
<li>Replace large loans with pay-as-you-go economics.</li>
<li>Modest returns on investment.</li>
<li>Give back to the community 30% of any windfall profit generated by land speculation.</li>
<li>Give up charettes and adopt more personal ways of talking to people.</li>
<li>Abandon the use of form-based prescriptive rules of geometry.</li>
<li>Professional fee-based Construction Management in lieu of large general contractors.</li>
<li>Diagnosis of the land as the start of every project.</li>
<li>Unfolding as the primary mode - hence cooperation with building and planning to accept this form.</li>
<li>Autonomy of every part at every scale allowed to develop its uniqueness.</li>
<li>Survey from stakes that are judged and placed and agreed by intuition.</li>
<li>Slow speed of construction to accommodate adaptation.</li>
<li>Fixed price construction and flexible moves within the budget.</li>
<li>Modify what is built, during construction.</li>
<li>Establish repair and maintenance programs for annual repair.</li>
</ol>
