<span class="h404class"> PROJECT ORIGINS: THE NOT-SO-GOOD, OLD WAY</span>

<img src="../pics/santarosa/construyamos.jpg" height="230" />\
<span class="caption">Colombian families working together under Construyamos to nail together each identical house as a very tiny standard box.</span>

<img src="../pics/santarosa/housing4.jpg" height="230" />\
<span class="caption">What the resulting "communities" of shacks look like -- this example from Soweto in South Africa.</span>

<span class="h4class">Originally our project in Santa Rosa started as a neighborhood for 70 families in the inland mountains of Colombia. We were asked to do it by Construyamos, one of the largest self-help housing cooperatives in Latin America, with an excellent track record of building mass-produced, low-cost housing. Their usual plan of action was to have eighteen families at a time, forming a group. An architect would draw up a standard small house plan (a modular shack, almost), repeated eighteen times, identically repeated. The families would work on all the houses together at the same time, by a kind of people's mass production. But no one knew which house was going to belong to which family. When the houses were all finished, the houses were assigned to the families by drawing numbers out of a hat.</span>

This was supposed to make sure everyone worked hard on all the houses equally; what it accomplished, more often, was an alienated feeling of creating a commodity, separated from the feeling of the families.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

<span class="h404class"> OUR PROJECT WITH CONSTRUYAMOS: A BETTER WAY</span>

<span class="h4class">When we began working with Hernan Mesa, the head of Construyamos, we persuaded him that we would manage a different process, in which the seventy families would lay out their neighborhood together; and we taught them, afterwards to lay out their individual houses, and then got them built, sometimes by self help, sometimes by paying masons and carpenters to help them. In short, with management by our volunteers, we taught the seventy families to build a neighborhood for themselves, in the mountains of central Colombia.</span>
