<td style="text-align: left;" width="500" data-valign="top" data-bgcolor="ffffdd"><blockquote>
<p><span class="h404class">The unfolding process happens </span></p>
<ol>
<li>IN PEOPLE'S MINDS: To some extent in people's minds -- in the minds of the people who live and work in the place, thinking about the neighborhood.</li>
<li>ON THE LAND: To some extent on the land itself, when people realize that certain new things should be in such and such a place, and mark it or remember it, and</li>
<li>ON DRAWINGS OF THE LAND: To a large extent it happens on a drawing of the site, which represents the land where action is to be taken.</li>
</ol>
<p>Since the third of these -- the drawings -- are very concrete, and very helpful, these are the places where one can most easily see the neighborhood developing and unfolding as a whole -- always provided that it is the first two considerations which govern what is drawn on this basemap.</p>
<p><br />
</p>

<td style="text-align: left;" width="500" data-valign="top" data-bgcolor="ffffdd"><blockquote>
<p><span class="h404class">It is on the basemap that you will most clearly witness, experience, and help to control the neighborhood process of unfolding, and see what results it brings. What follows is a list of steps to take on the drawing pad, in roughly the order you should take them.</span></p>
<p>Each new drawing you make is to be coupled with a particular unfolding. And each gets its own layer. Its name will appear in the list of layers at the left hand edge of the map.</p>
<ol>
<li><span style="color:blue">Aerial Photo layer</span> <span style="font-weight:normal">Get an aerial photo of the land, and place this photo in a file of your choosing, on your own computer. Make this your first layer, and call it <span style="color:blue">Aerial photo</span></span>.</li>
<li><span style="color:blue">Basemap layer (1.4).</span><span style="font-weight:normal"> Make another layer, above the aerial photo, and make all kinds of marks to show things that are not clear in the aerial -- things that make it easier to grasp recognizable features like roads, buildings, trees, and landmarks. Call this second layer <span style="color:blue">Basemap.</span>. You may correct the basemap over time. As you recognize that you need more detail in a particular place, go out and pace it off or measure it, and then correct the drawing on this layer to reflect the real situation more exactly.</span></li>
<li><span style="color:blue">Diagnosis layer (2.5).</span><span style="font-weight:normal">Now, by following the unfolding called Diagnosis map, you can draw important or beautiful views, special small places, special public places, natural paths which suggest themselves, valuable trees, particularly nice buildings which may play an important role in the future growth of the neighborhood, and so on. Call this layer <span style="color:blue">Diagnosis</span>. You can add to the diagnosis layer gradually as you get to know what matters in the site.</span></li>
<li><p>to be continued...</p>
<p><br />
</p></li>
</ol>
